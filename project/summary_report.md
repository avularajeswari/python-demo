                         Employee Data Cleaning and Exploratory Data Analysis
1. Problem Statement
Employee datasets may contain missing values, duplicate records, inconsistent formats, and incorrect entries that affect the reliability of HR reporting. This project aims to clean employee data and perform Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) to identify workforce patterns and support data-driven HR decisions.
2. Data and Methodology
The project uses an employee dataset containing information such as employee ID, age, gender, department, job role, salary, joining date, and experience, depending on the available columns.
Data cleaning involves identifying missing values, removing duplicate records, standardizing column names and data formats, checking inconsistent categories, and validating numerical values. EDA uses descriptive statistics, frequency distributions, and visualizations to explore employee characteristics and workforce trends.
3. Three Key Insights
1.	Workforce distribution: Analyze which departments have the highest and lowest employee counts to understand how employees are distributed across the organization.
2.	Salary patterns: Compare salary distributions across departments, job roles, or experience levels to identify differences in compensation.
3.	Employee demographics and experience: Examine age, tenure, or experience distributions to understand workforce composition and identify potential patterns.

4. Recommendations
1.	Improve data quality: Establish consistent data-entry standards, validation rules, and regular checks for missing values, duplicate records, and inconsistent formats.
2.	Use data-driven workforce planning: Use the observed department, salary, and experience patterns to guide further HR investigation and workforce planning, while validating findings before making policy decisions.
5. Limitations
The analysis depends on the completeness, accuracy, and representativeness of the available dataset. Missing information, outdated records, limited variables, and potential data-entry errors may affect the results. EDA identifies patterns and associations but does not establish causation. Employee information should also be handled confidentially and in accordance with applicable privacy requirements.
